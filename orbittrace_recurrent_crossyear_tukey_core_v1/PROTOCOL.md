## 3. Cross-year nearest-support statistic

For each frozen Recurrent parent `P_r`, independently:

1. split its frozen members into `P_r,2022` and `P_r,2023` using the target-excluded geometry export;
2. for every member `x`, compute `d_cross(x)`, the exact Euclidean distance in the frozen six-coordinate embedding to its nearest **opposite-year member of the same frozen parent**;
3. pool `d_cross(x)` over all members of that parent;
4. compute `Q1` and `Q3` as NumPy linear quantiles at exactly `0.25` and `0.75`;
5. define `IQR = Q3 - Q1` and the fixed conventional Tukey upper fence

`F_r = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR`.

The coefficient `1.5` is fixed here before any outcome and is the classical Tukey outer-halo screening convention. It may not be changed, swept, scaled by family size, or compared with another multiplier.

## 4. Exact membership rule

Define the raw support core

`C_r = { x in P_r : d_cross(x) <= F_r }`.

The successor slot at rank `r` is:

- `C_r` iff `C_r` contains at least 4 events from 2022 **and** at least 4 events from 2023;
- otherwise the original parent `P_r` unchanged.

The support-4 annual floor is inherited from the existing sparse meteor-stream reporting semantics; it is not selected from this result.

Properties by construction:

- successor membership is always a subset of the same-rank parent;
- no event can enter from another family or from outside the parent;

- removed events never cause recomputation, recursion, re-centering, or additional pruning.

There is no lower fence. Small cross-year distance is evidence of corroboration, not an anomaly to remove.
