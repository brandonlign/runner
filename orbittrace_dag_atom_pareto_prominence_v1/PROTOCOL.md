## 5. Reused two-view Pareto-prominence order

The ordering deliberately reuses the already-frozen and successful Pareto-prominence principle from recurrent–TopoModal Pareto-prominence v1 rather than inventing a new truth-informed fusion rule.

For each atom `a`, define two ordinal objectives, both minimized.

### Objective A — recurrence priority

`R(a) = rank of recurrent parent R_j`.

This is the exact recurrent-EOM rank frozen in the DAG source.

### Objective B — intrinsic TopoModal prominence rank

Consider only distinct TopoModal parents that contribute at least one atom in the panel.

Order those contributing TopoModal parents exactly by the pre-existing Pareto-prominence rule:

1. descending `modal_contrast`;
2. ascending frozen `native_support_rank`;
3. ascending `family_hash`.

Let `M(T_i)` be the one-indexed position in this deterministic order. Every atom from `T_i` receives

`M(a) = M(T_i)`.

This is the exact analogue of the earlier uniquely corroborated-child modal rank, except the DAG now permits one TopoModal child to contribute multiple atoms to different recurrent parents instead of requiring unique ownership.

No normalization, coefficient, learned score, quantile, atom size, degree, year, denominator, or bucket enters either objective.

### Pareto depth

For two distinct atoms `a,b`, `a` dominates `b` iff:

- `R(a) <= R(b)` and `M(a) <= M(b)`, and
- at least one inequality is strict.

Assign ordinary non-dominated layers by repeated frontier removal until every atom has exactly one positive integer layer `L(a)`.

### Final deterministic total order

Order all atoms lexicographically by:

1. ascending `L(a)`;
2. ascending `M(a)`;
3. ascending `R(a)`;
4. ascending frozen TopoModal `native_support_rank`;
5. ascending `atom_hash`.

All atoms are retained exactly once.

The key dense-regime behavior is intentional: when one TopoModal child overlaps several recurrent parents, its atoms share the same intrinsic prominence rank but receive different recurrence ranks. The stronger recurrent corroboration can therefore dominate the weaker split **without deleting it or forcing a unique parent**.
