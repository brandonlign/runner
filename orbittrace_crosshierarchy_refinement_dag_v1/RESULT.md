## Interpretation

The exact common-refinement representation succeeds at the question it was designed to answer: representing all nonempty TopoModal↔recurrent overlaps, rather than forcing unique ownership, gives a catalogue geometry that is more stable under deterministic thinning than either parent partition on its own.

The gain over TopoModal is modest in aggregate (`+0.01485` pooled mean; `+0.01320` median) but consistent enough to pass the deliberately strict 7/8 transition test. The gain over recurrent-EOM is much larger (`+0.11485` pooled mean).

This is structural evidence only. It does not show that raw atoms are good shower candidates, that they improve known-shower recovery, or that any particular atom ranking is useful. Atomization may fragment physically coherent showers; the next authorized experiment must therefore define a detector catalogue and ranking *before* opening shower truth, then test that frozen catalogue against the established GMN development truth gates.
